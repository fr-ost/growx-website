# Answer bank for Reddit, Quora and forums

Fifteen answers to questions people ask about X (Twitter) all the time. Each one is meant to solve the problem on its own, even if
nobody clicks the link. The link is a bonus for people who want more.

## Rules (read before posting)

1. **Answer only where the question is genuinely asked.** Never post a question yourself, from any account, to answer it. No alt
   accounts, no asking friends to upvote.
2. **Follow each community's rules on self-promotion.** Read the sidebar or posting guidelines first. Many subreddits ban links to your
   own product or allow them only in certain threads. If links are not allowed, post the answer without the link, or skip the thread.
3. **Never post the same text twice.** These are starting points, not copy-paste replies. Identical answers across threads look like
   spam to moderators and to X-related communities alike, and they get removed.
4. **Adapt every answer to the thread.** Start with the asker's situation (their niche, account size, what they already tried), drop the
   parts that do not apply, and answer any follow-up questions in the comments.
5. **Always keep the disclosure line** when the answer mentions GrowX or links to growxapp.org.
6. **One link at most.** If the thread already has a good answer, add something new or move on.
7. **Check facts before posting.** X changes features often (for example, scheduling in the composer). The GrowX facts here match
   extension v2.4.4; see `docs/PRODUCT_FACTS.md`.
8. **Log each answer** in the tracker in `docs/BACKLINKS.md` (section 8).

Where these questions come up: Reddit (for example r/Twitter, r/socialmedia, r/SocialMediaMarketing, r/Entrepreneur and niche

## 1. Who unfollowed me on Twitter?

**How people ask it:** "How do I see who unfollowed me on Twitter?" / "Does X notify you when someone unfollows you?" / "Is there an app to
see who unfollowed me on X?"
**Link:** https://www.growxapp.org/blog/who-unfollowed-me-on-twitter

**Answer (154 words):**

X doesn't notify you when someone unfollows, and there's no built-in list of unfollowers. Your options:

1. **Check one person.** Open their profile. If you don't see "Follows you" next to their handle, they aren't following you.
2. **Compare lists by hand.** Save your follower list (screenshots or a spreadsheet), check again a week later and see who's missing. Fine
   for small accounts, painful once you have a few hundred followers.
3. **Use a tracker.** A tracker snapshots your followers on a schedule and shows the difference: who followed and who left since the last
   check. Avoid any tool that asks for your X password. Trackers that work inside your logged-in browser, or use X's official sign-in,
   don't need it.

One tip: don't take single unfollows personally. The useful signal is a pattern, such as a cluster of unfollows after a certain kind of
post or after a day when you posted a lot.

I wrote a longer guide, including how the GrowX tracker works (7 days of history on the free plan): https://www.growxapp.org/blog/who-unfollowed-me-on-twitter

## 2. What is the best time to post on X?

**How people ask it:** "What's the best time to post on Twitter?" / "When should I tweet to get the most views?" / "Best time to post on X
in 2026?"
**Link:** https://www.growxapp.org/blog/best-time-to-post-on-twitter

**Answer (161 words):**

There isn't one best time for everyone. The charts you see online describe other people's audiences, and yours may live in a different time
zone or keep different hours. You can find your own in a few weeks:

1. For two or three weeks, post at a few different times, say morning, lunch and evening in your audience's main time zone.
2. For each post, note the hour and its engagement rate: likes + reposts + replies + bookmarks, divided by views. Use the rate, not raw
   likes, so posts with different reach compare fairly.
3. Group posts by time block and compare the medians. Keep the blocks that beat your usual rate, and test a new one now and then.

Check where your followers actually are, too. If most of them are a few time zones away, their evening might be your night.

Fresh posts get their best chance in the first hours, so post when you can be around to answer replies.

Step-by-step guide: https://www.growxapp.org/blog/best-time-to-post-on-twitter

## 3. How do I schedule tweets for free?

**How people ask it:** "How to schedule tweets for free?" / "Is there a free Twitter scheduler?" / "Can you schedule posts on X without
paying?"
**Link:** https://www.growxapp.org/blog/how-to-schedule-tweets

**Answer (152 words):**

A few free routes, depending on how much you post:

- **X's own composer.** On the web, depending on your account, the composer may show a schedule option (a small calendar icon next to the
  media buttons). Fine for the odd post. X Pro, the dashboard that replaced TweetDeck, also schedules, but it's tied to a paid X
  subscription.
- **Free plans of scheduling tools.** Most cap how many posts can wait in the queue at once, so check that number first. GrowX's free plan,
  for example, allows 10 posts or threads waiting at a time.
- **Browser-based schedulers** post through your logged-in browser, so your computer has to be on at posting time. The upside is that they
  can work without your password.

The tool matters less than the habit: write several posts in one sitting, then drop them into fixed time slots (morning, midday, evening).
Preview threads and images before you schedule them.

Full walkthrough: https://www.growxapp.org/blog/how-to-schedule-tweets

## 4. Do links count toward the 280 characters?

**How people ask it:** "Do links count in the Twitter character limit?" / "How many characters does a URL take in a tweet?" / "Why does
X say my tweet is too long?"
**Link:** https://www.growxapp.org/tools/tweet-character-counter

**Answer (130 words):**

Yes, but not at their full length. X wraps every link in its own short link, so each link counts as **23 characters**, however long or
short the real URL is. A 120-character URL uses 23, and so does a tiny one.

Other counting rules that catch people out:

- Most emoji count as 2 characters, including emoji with skin tones.
- Chinese, Japanese and Korean characters count as 2 each.
- "Bold" or "italic" letters from text generators are special Unicode characters and count as 2 each.
- Standard posts get 280 characters. Accounts with X Premium can write long posts of up to 25,000.
- Attached images don't use any characters.

If you're just over, cut filler words ("really", "just", "I think") or move the extra part into a reply or a thread.

Paste a draft into this free counter, which follows X's rules, to see the exact count: https://www.growxapp.org/tools/tweet-character-counter

## 5. Why can't I follow more people on Twitter?

**How people ask it:** "Why can't I follow anyone on Twitter?" / "X says I'm unable to follow more people" / "What is the Twitter follow
limit?"
**Link:** https://www.growxapp.org/tools/twitter-follower-ratio-calculator

**Answer (145 words):**

Usually it's one of two limits:

1. **The following cap.** Everyone can follow up to 5,000 accounts. Past that, how many you can follow depends on how many people follow
   you. X doesn't publish the formula and it can vary, but a common working estimate is about 10% above your follower count. So with 5,000
   following and 1,200 followers, you're stuck until your followers grow or you unfollow some accounts.
2. **Rate limits.** X also limits how many accounts you can follow per day and watches short bursts. Newer accounts tend to be treated more
   strictly. If you hit one, stop for the day; pushing on is what leads to locks.

What helps: unfollow inactive accounts and people who never followed back, in small batches rather than hundreds at once. Use Lists to read
people without following them. And make your profile worth following back.

This calculator shows your ratio and how far you are from the cap: https://www.growxapp.org/tools/twitter-follower-ratio-calculator

## 6. How do I write in bold on Twitter?

**How people ask it:** "How to bold text on Twitter?" / "How do people get bold and italic letters in their X bio?" / "Can you use bold in
a tweet?"
**Link:** https://www.growxapp.org/tools/twitter-bold-text-generator

**Answer (153 words):**

X has no bold button for regular posts, but you can still get bold-looking text. Unicode includes full alphabets that look bold, italic or
script (they were made for maths). Because they're real characters, they keep their look when you paste them into a post, reply or bio. A
bold text generator swaps each letter for its styled twin, and you copy the result.

Before you use it everywhere:

- Each styled letter counts as **2 characters** on X, so bold eats into your 280 fast.
- Screen readers may read styled letters badly or not at all, which shuts out people who rely on them.
- Styled words may not match searches, so keep names and keywords in plain text.
- Accented letters, emoji and punctuation usually have no styled version.

Best practice: bold a word or two for emphasis, like a heading in a thread or one key phrase in your bio, not whole sentences.

Free generator that runs in your browser: https://www.growxapp.org/tools/twitter-bold-text-generator

## 7. What is a good engagement rate on Twitter?

**How people ask it:** "What's a good engagement rate on X?" / "Is a 1% engagement rate on Twitter bad?" / "How do you calculate Twitter
engagement rate?"
**Link:** https://www.growxapp.org/tools/twitter-engagement-rate-calculator

**Answer (141 words):**

Honest answer: there's no single benchmark worth trusting. Engagement rate swings with niche, account size and type of post, so a number
from someone else's account, or a generic industry average, says little about yours.

What's more useful:

1. **Calculate it per view:** (likes + reposts + replies + bookmarks) / views x 100. For example, 40 engagements on 2,000 views is 2%. Per
   view is fairer than per follower, because many viewers don't follow you.
2. **Find your own median** over your last 20 to 30 posts. Use the median, not the average, so one viral post doesn't skew it.
3. **Study the posts clearly above your median.** What topic, format, hook or posting time do they share?
4. **Track the median monthly.** A rising median is the real sign of progress.

If views are missing, dividing by followers works as a rough fallback.

Free calculator (per view and per follower): https://www.growxapp.org/tools/twitter-engagement-rate-calculator

## 8. How do I write a Twitter thread?

**How people ask it:** "How to make a thread on Twitter?" / "How do you write a good X thread?" / "How do I connect tweets into a thread?"
**Link:** https://www.growxapp.org/tools/twitter-thread-maker

**Answer (170 words):**

A structure that works for most topics:

1. **Hook first.** Make a clear promise: what the reader gets and why it's worth their time. "7 mistakes I made pricing my first product,
   so you can skip them:" beats "A thread on pricing".
2. **One idea per post.** Each post should make sense on its own, since people repost single parts.
3. **Number the posts** (1/, 2/ ...) if it helps readers see the length, and be consistent.
4. **Split at natural breaks**, at the end of a sentence or paragraph, never mid-sentence. Leave room for the numbering, and remember links
   count as 23 characters.
5. **End with a wrap-up**: a short summary and one ask (follow, read more, or reply with their own tips).

To post it, write the first post, press the "+" in the composer to add each next part, then publish them together. Draft the whole thing
in a doc first so you can edit it as one piece. And don't pad: five strong posts beat fifteen weak ones.

Free tool that splits long text into numbered posts: https://www.growxapp.org/tools/twitter-thread-maker

(Disclosure: I build GrowX.)

## 9. How do I get my first 1,000 followers?

**How people ask it:** "How to get your first 1000 followers on Twitter?" / "How do small accounts grow on X?" / "I have 50 followers,
what am I doing wrong?"
**Link:** https://www.growxapp.org/blog/first-1000-followers-on-x

**Answer (164 words):**

Nobody can promise a number, but here's what I'd focus on as a small account:

1. **Fix your profile first.** Your bio says who it's for and what you post, and your best post is pinned. When someone clicks your name
   after a good reply, that page has to convince them.
2. **Pick two or three topics** and stick with them, so people know what they'll get.
3. **Reply early, every day.** Find posts from the last hour or two by bigger accounts in your niche and reply with something that adds
   value: an example, a counterpoint, a question. For small accounts this is often the biggest lever, because everyone reading already
   cares about your topic.
4. **Post consistently** at a pace you can keep for months.
5. **Follow relevant people** a few at a time, not hundreds in bursts, which looks like spam.
6. **Review weekly** which posts and replies brought followers, and do more of that.

It's slow at first. It compounds.

Week-by-week plan: https://www.growxapp.org/blog/first-1000-followers-on-x

## 10. Am I shadowbanned on X?

**How people ask it:** "Am I shadowbanned on Twitter?" / "How do I check if I'm shadowbanned on X?" / "My views dropped to nothing, is it
a shadowban?"
**Link:** https://www.growxapp.org/blog/twitter-shadowban

**Answer (170 words):**

X doesn't officially use the word, but it does limit the visibility of some accounts and posts: leaving them out of search suggestions,
excluding posts from search, ranking replies lower or showing posts to fewer people. First rule out normal dips: reach moves with topic,
timing and whatever else is happening on X.

Check it yourself (shadowban checker sites only see the same public views you do):

1. From an account that doesn't follow you (a friend's or a second one), type your name in search and check the suggestions.
2. Search an exact phrase from a recent post, in quotes, under Latest.
3. Open a conversation you replied to and see whether your reply is visible.
4. Check your notifications and email for any notice from X.

If something is limited: pause automation, stop follow and unfollow churn, mass tagging and copy-paste replies, post normally and give it
time. X doesn't say how long limits last. Don't open a new account to get around it; X's rules prohibit that.

More detail: https://www.growxapp.org/blog/twitter-shadowban

## 11. What should I put in my Twitter bio?

**How people ask it:** "What to write in a Twitter bio?" / "Good X bio ideas?" / "How do I make my Twitter bio stand out?"
**Link:** https://www.growxapp.org/blog/twitter-bio-ideas
**Alternative link** (when the asker wants a quick generator rather than ideas): https://www.growxapp.org/tools/twitter-bio-generator

**Answer (159 words):**

You get 160 characters, and most good bios answer four questions:

1. **Who it's for.** Name the audience: early-stage founders, new runners, junior developers.
2. **What you post.** Your two or three recurring topics. This is the part most bios skip, and it's what tells a visitor whether to follow.
3. **Proof.** One specific, true reason to listen: a role, something you built, years doing it.
4. **One call to action.** Read the pinned thread, join the newsletter, see your work.

A template: "I help {audience} {result}. Posts on {topic 1} and {topic 2}. {proof}. {call to action}". Write a long version, then cut until
it fits.

Habits to drop: a list of five job titles, vague words like "passionate" or "enthusiast", walls of emoji or hashtags, and inside jokes new
visitors won't get. Then check the rest of the profile: a clear photo, a header that shows what you do and a pinned post with your best work.

Bio examples by niche: https://www.growxapp.org/blog/twitter-bio-ideas

## 12. How often should I tweet?

**How people ask it:** "How many times a day should I tweet?" / "How often should you post on X to grow?" / "Is tweeting too much bad?"
**Link:** https://www.growxapp.org/blog/how-often-to-post-on-twitter

**Answer (159 words):**

There's no magic number. Anyone who gives you one is describing what worked for their account. Consistency and quality matter more:

- Pick a pace you can keep for months, not just a week. For many people that's a few posts a week or one or two a day, plus daily replies.
- Count replies as part of your presence. For small accounts, thoughtful replies on fresh posts in their niche are often the main way new
  people find them.
- Spread posts out instead of posting five in ten minutes, which crowds your own posts and can look automated.
- Batch-write and schedule on busy weeks, so the streak doesn't break.

How to tell if you're posting too much: your engagement rate per view keeps falling as volume rises, or you notice more unfollows after busy
days. Too little: weeks go by with no new followers and almost no conversations. In that case, add a daily reply habit before adding more
posts.

Guide with a weekly routine: https://www.growxapp.org/blog/how-often-to-post-on-twitter

## 13. Does follow for follow work?

**How people ask it:** "Does F4F work on Twitter?" / "Should I follow back everyone?" / "Are follow trains worth it on X?"
**Link:** https://www.growxapp.org/blog/follow-for-follow-twitter

**Answer (163 words):**

It can raise your follower count, but usually not in a way that helps. Follow for follow trades follows with people who agreed to follow
back, mostly regardless of what you post. The problems:

- **They're not your audience.** They followed for the trade, not your content, so they rarely read, reply or repost. Your engagement drops
  relative to your follower count.
- **Churn.** Some F4F accounts unfollow after you follow back, leaving your following count high and your ratio worse.
- **Spam patterns.** Mass following and unfollowing in bursts, follow trains and "follow everyone who likes this" posts are the kind of
  behaviour X's platform manipulation rules describe, and they can get an account limited.

Targeted following works better: follow real, active people in your niche, a few at a time, especially people who engage with accounts
like yours. Some will follow back because they're actually interested. Give them a few days, then quietly unfollow those who didn't, unless
you follow them for their content.

More on this: https://www.growxapp.org/blog/follow-for-follow-twitter

## 14. How do I use X Lists?

**How people ask it:** "What are Twitter Lists for?" / "How do I make a List on X?" / "Are Twitter Lists still worth using?"
**Link:** https://www.growxapp.org/blog/twitter-lists-guide

**Answer (169 words):**

A List is a group of accounts with its own timeline that shows only their posts. You can add anyone with a public account, even people you
don't follow.

To make one: open Lists from the menu on x.com or in the app, create a List, name it, choose public or private, then add members by search
or from any profile's menu.

Good uses:

- **A niche feed** of 30 to 50 accounts in your topic, so you see their posts even when For you is noisy.
- **A reply list** of people whose posts you want to reply to early. Check it once or twice a day.
- **Following less.** Read people in a List instead of following them, which keeps your following count and ratio healthier.
- **Research** on competitors, prospects or people at an event.

Private Lists are visible only to you, and nobody is notified when you add them. Anyone can see and follow public Lists, and people can see
the public Lists they're on, so name those carefully.

Full guide with List ideas: https://www.growxapp.org/blog/twitter-lists-guide

## 15. How does the X algorithm work?

**How people ask it:** "How does the Twitter algorithm work in 2026?" / "What does the X algorithm favour?" / "How do I get on the For you
page?"
**Link:** https://www.growxapp.org/blog/twitter-algorithm-explained

**Answer (167 words):**

Nobody outside X knows the current details, so be sceptical of anyone quoting exact weights. What's public: in 2023, X published parts of
its recommendation code and described how the For you feed is built, in three stages:

1. **Candidate sourcing:** gather recent posts from accounts you follow and accounts you don't, based on who and what you engage with.
2. **Ranking:** a model predicts how likely you are to engage with each post (reply, repost, like, spend time on it).
3. **Filtering and mixing:** remove blocked or muted accounts and rule-breaking content, keep the feed varied, add ads.

That was a snapshot, and the system has changed since. Takeaways that still hold: write posts that invite real replies, and answer them;
post when your audience is online, since fresh posts get their best chance early; reply early to others in your niche; stay in a clear
niche so the system can find your readers; and avoid spammy patterns like follow churn, repeated posts and hashtag stuffing.

Longer explainer: https://www.growxapp.org/blog/twitter-algorithm-explained

(Disclosure: I build GrowX.)
